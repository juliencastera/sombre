Reading at night without burning your eyes

Camille Laurent · 8 min read · Updated October 2026

Most of the web is still designed for daylight: white pages, light grey captions, thin borders. At night, a single white tab is enough to wake you up, and the small grey text that looked elegant at noon becomes unreadable.

A good dark mode is not an inverted page: colors keep their meaning, and text stays readable.

Dark themes work best when every surface, border and shadow is recomputed, not just the background. Here is the kind of rule a reading mode has to understand: 

.article {
  background: #ffffff;
  color: #1f2328;  /* light page, dark text */
}

Contrast matters even more than color. Captions, timestamps and placeholders are often below any readable threshold, and they are exactly what you look for when you skim.
